Local AI and Possible Misses

S2 Watch works without AI, using keyword rules. With Ollama on the same computer it can read meaning as well. Nothing leaves your computer.

Setting it up

  1. Install Ollama and run ollama pull llama3.2.
  2. Configuration → Analysis Mode → Local Ollama with rules fallback.
  3. Optional: Analysis Mode → Ollama settings… to change the model or endpoint (default http://127.0.0.1:11434).
  4. For assessing events, also run ollama pull qwen2.5:7b (about 4.7 GB). It is the default Event assessment model in Ollama settings.

What it does

  • The rules read every message first; only radio messages they flag go to the model, which keeps S2 Watch quick on a computer without a suitable graphics card (about 9 seconds a message). Official feeds stay with the rules.
  • The model drops chat the rules mistook for news, e.g. “How often do you feel the earthquakes around your area?”, and writes clearer headlines.
  • In report drafts, Suggest assessment (Ollama) drafts the analysis sections (see Assessing an event below). Check every figure: drafts round and embellish.
  • If Ollama stops answering, S2 Watch falls back to the rules and says so in the status bar.

Assessing an event

To start one, select an event (in the list under the map with Show: Events, or on the Events tab), then any of:

  • the Assess (Ollama) button under the event's details;
  • right-click the event → Assess event (Ollama);
  • Reports → Assess Selected Event (Ollama), or Ctrl+Shift+A.

The button reads Assessing… while it works, and the result appears at the top of the event's details.

The assessment asks the model what the event's reports add up to. It reads the first report and the latest six (numbered as in the panel), the official confirmation and the Background facts, and writes a suggestion shown at the top of the panel, apart from the facts:

  • BLUF, the current situation first;
  • Trend: escalating, stable, winding down or unclear, and why;
  • Agreement: whether the reports agree or contradict each other;
  • assessment paragraphs in measured language, a Probability table (HIGH / MODERATE / LOW), Intelligence gaps, Mitigation and MAGNET guidance.

Every statement cites the reports it rests on, e.g. [22]; check them. The model is told to use only facts in the reports, but it can still assume things (a landfall, a direction) the reports don't say. It never changes an event or a report. The assessment is saved; when new reports arrive it shows ⚠ Out of date until you assess again.

Accepting an assessment

Ollama's result is a proposed assessment, marked not yet accepted. To make it the event's assessment:

  1. Click Review & accept… under it. Each section opens as a field: BLUF, trend and why, agreement, assessment (a blank line between paragraphs), probability (one per line, what may happen | HIGH), gaps, mitigation and MAGNET guidance.
  2. Check each statement against the numbered reports, correct it, add what you know, and click Accept.

It becomes the event's Analyst assessment, shown at the top of its details with who accepted it and when (Ollama-drafted (qwen2.5:7b), reviewed by N1ROG). Dismiss drops a proposal you don't want. With no assessment yet, write one starts an empty one of your own (written by N1ROG); edit changes the accepted one.

When new reports arrive, the accepted assessment shows ⚠ Out of date. Assess again: the new result appears below yours as a Proposed update, with What changed (trend, probability levels, new gaps, newer reports read). Accept it (after editing), or dismiss it. Every accepted version is kept: history lists them, newest first, so a handover can see when the picture changed.

The accepted assessment is what the rest of S2 Watch uses:

  • the events lists mark the event's title with its trend: ▲ escalating, ▬ stable, ▼ winding down, ? unclear;
  • a MAGNET S2 draft of the event fills its analytical sections from it (an unreviewed proposal is used only when nothing is accepted, and the draft says so; an assessment older than the latest reports says how many it read);
  • a shift briefing lists each assessed event's trend, when and by whom, and its BLUF under COMMENTS / ASSESSMENT, with up to two of its probability lines in the table.

Example. You accept Vance as ▼ winding down after deleting “moving towards Mexico”. Six hours later two new advisories arrive: the assessment shows ⚠ Out of date: 2 new reports since. Assess again: the proposed update reads What changed: Reads 2 newer reports; New gap: Any coastal effects in Colima?. Accept it; your earlier version stays in history.

It takes about two minutes on a computer without a suitable graphics card, in the background. It uses the Event assessment model (Ollama settings; default qwen2.5:7b): a 7–8B model sticks to the reports, while the 3B model used for messages misread trends and invented impacts in testing. To protect the rest of the computer, S2 Watch first unloads other models, checks there is enough free memory (about 8 GB for qwen2.5:7b; otherwise it says so and doesn't start), and unloads the model as soon as it answers. Close browser tabs if it reports too little memory.

In a MAGNET S2 draft of that event, a current assessment fills COMMENTS / ASSESSMENT (with the trend and the probability table), INTELLIGENCE GAPS, MITIGATION RECOMMENDATIONS and MAGNET GUIDANCE, citing the draft's SOURCE LIST numbers. Suggest assessment (Ollama) in the draft runs a fresh one.

Example: Tropical Storm Vance, 22 NHC reports.

Trend: winding down — intensity has fallen since its peak [18, 19, 20, 21].
BLUF: Tropical Storm Vance holds 55 kt winds and 997 mb, moving WNW at 7 mph [22].
Probability: Continued 55 kt winds | MODERATE
Gaps: No reports mention rainfall amounts or coastal effects.

Possible Misses

Tick Analysis Mode → Check unmatched radio traffic in the background (Ollama). The model then reads, slowly, the radio messages no watch form kept, skipping heartbeats, CQs, signal reports and exercise or test traffic, and lists anything it thinks is news in the Possible Misses tab. It uses at most about a third of the computer's time.

Example. W5ABC: TREES ACROSS HWY 9 EAST OF ADA, ONE LANE OPEN wasn't kept: none of the Infrastructure keywords matched. It shows under Possible Misses as Infrastructure. Add as report… lets you correct the category, urgency and headline, and add a keyword (here trees across) to the Infrastructure topic, so the next such message is caught. Your corrections are kept for good: later checks never overwrite them. Dismiss hides one that isn't news; Restore and Delete manage dismissed items. Your decisions are given to the model as examples, so it learns what this network counts as news.
