Official confirmation and radio lead

A radio report is confirmed when an official source describes the same kind of hazard, close to the same place, at about the same time. The Official column of the Reports list and the event panel then show it.

Example. ✓ NWS Flood Warning · radio 45 min earlier — an active NWS Flood Warning covers the report's location, and our network reported the flooding 45 minutes before the warning was issued.

  • Radio lead is how long before the official source our network reported it. Radio lead compares when the reports were heard, not the time a message says something happened.
  • News websites never confirm; they are open-source coverage. An intelligence entry confirms only when entered as coming from an official source.
  • A blank means unconfirmed, not false. Many real reports (outages, supply problems, local incidents) have no official counterpart.
  • The report's location must be specific (coordinates, grid square, town or county) for a match, except a FEMA declaration, which confirms anything placed in its state.
