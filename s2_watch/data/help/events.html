Events and the event panel

An event is one real happening, with every report about it: a StatRep, a JS8Call message, an NWS warning and a FEMA declaration about one flood are one event.

How reports join an event

  • Same category and kind of hazard (flooding with flooding, not with a fire nearby);
  • within 48 hours of the event's latest report;
  • within about 100 km (150 km for earthquakes, 250 km for hurricanes), or the same state when only the state is known.

A named official incident (a NIFC fire, a USGS quake, a hurricane, a river gauge) stays one event however long it lasts. Stories without a physical hazard (crime, politics) must also share distinctive words. When unsure, S2 Watch keeps reports apart: one story shown as two events is safer than two incidents merged.

Status

StatusMeans
ActiveReports are still coming in.
Quiet 14 hNo new report for 14 hours (the threshold is set under Configuration → Display…, 12 hours by default). It may be over.
Closed: power restoredA report said it is over, or you closed it. Closed events are greyed; the Events tab can hide them.

Example: an event closing itself.

14:05Z  KD0ABC: TULSA OK - POWER OUTAGE ACROSS NORTH TULSA
16:40Z  KD0ABC: TULSA OK - POWER HAS BEEN RESTORED, OUTAGE OVER

The second report joins the event and closes it: Closed: power has been restored. If a third report says POWER OUTAGE AGAIN NORTH TULSA, the event reopens.

Words that close an event: all clear, power restored, warning cancelled, evacuation order lifted, bridge reopened, 100% contained, suspect in custody, net secured. A negation keeps it open: POWER NOT YET RESTORED does not close it. Only radio and official reports close events; a news headline such as “suspects arrested” never does.

The event panel

Select an event (on the Map tab or the Events tab) and the panel shows:

  • category, area, urgency, when it was heard and how many reports from which sources;
  • its status, and official confirmation with the radio lead (Official confirmation);
  • warnings: ⚠ Single source or 📡 Radio silence nearby (below);
  • your labels and notes;
  • every report, newest first: full text, sender and its track record, the time the message gives, its own confirmation, a link for official and news items, where it was placed (the state or country and the point's latitude and longitude; a storm far out at sea shows its coordinates alone), and a fix location link.

Reports are numbered in the order heard ([1] is the first), so an assessment's citations can be checked. An event you have assessed shows its trend before its title in the lists: ▲ escalating, ▬ stable, ▼ winding down, ? unclear (Accepting an assessment).

Buttons: Show on map, Draft follow-up…, Assess (Ollama) (what the reports add up to; see Local AI), Label, Show its reports, Close event… / Reopen event. Right-clicking events in either list gives the same actions for several at once.

Searching

The Search box above the list on the Map tab (for events or reports, whichever is shown) and on the Events tab keeps the rows containing every word you type, in any order; capital letters don't matter. For an event it looks in the title, category, area, sources, labels, status and the full text, senders and notes of all its reports. While you search, every match is shown, however far down the list; clear the box (the ✕) for the usual list.

Examples.

tornado            → every event with “tornado” in it or in any of its reports
kd0abc flood       → flood events KD0ABC reported
closed tulsa       → closed events around Tulsa
follow-up S2-2026  → events you labelled with that follow-up number

“tornado” also finds an event titled “Damaging wind — North Carolina” when one of its reports mentions a tornado.

Example: single source. Only W2XYZ has reported a bridge collapse, and two hours later no other station and no official source has said so. The panel shows ⚠ Single source: only one station or outlet has reported this, and nothing has confirmed it in 2 hours, and a briefing asks “Can another station or an official source confirm …?” A second station's report, or an official item, clears it. News stories alone are never flagged: most are one article.

Example: radio silence. A tornado event sits near grid square EM26, whose stations normally send about 6 messages every 3 hours. They have sent nothing for 5 hours. The panel shows 📡 Radio silence nearby: stations around EM26 (KD0ABC, W5XYZ) usually send about 6 messages every 3 hours and haven't been heard for 5 hours. Silence is only reported next to an open event: on its own it is usually just a lull.
